Resilience in Action

What watching my son taught me about resilience, drive, ownership and leadership.

Adapted from a series of LinkedIn posts.

Guy BavaroOctober 12, 2026

Resilience.

It’s a word we use often in leadership discussions, and for good reason.

Resilience is what enables people to navigate challenges, adapt to change and keep moving when things get hard. For the teams I engage with, I emphasize it. And in every hire, I look for it.

Why?

Because in my experience, resilient people are often the ones who surprise you the most.

They get knocked down and stand back up. They adjust. They take ownership. They stay committed to the goal even when the path changes.

I’ve witnessed that quality in a lot of people and situations, but one example I feel most deeply is my son, Gaetano, who we call Tommy.

Resilience and drive are different

Tommy has both.

Resilience keeps you standing when challenges show up.

Drive pushes you forward after you’re back on your feet.

He has spent more than a decade pursuing competitive mogul skiing. That means freezing days on the side of a mountain, two-a-day workouts away from the snow, and a competition schedule that puts years of preparation in front of judges and spectators in a matter of seconds.

The physical part is obvious.

The mental part is harder to see.

Every run is public. Every mistake is visible. And after every result, you have to decide what you’re going to do next.

What 24 hours can teach you

One weekend at Utah Olympic Park made the lesson especially clear to me.

On Saturday, Tommy finished ninth out of more than 50 male athletes.

He was frustrated.

He knew he could do better.

But instead of allowing the disappointment to take over, he analyzed the run, looked at what he could control and made adjustments.

That night he said something to me that stuck:

“Growth comes from taking ownership and what I can control.”

He was 18.

The next day, he won first place in singles and finished third in duals. Two podium finishes in one day.

The result was great.

But what impressed me most was what happened between Saturday and Sunday.

Frustration became analysis.

Analysis became adjustment.

Adjustment became execution.

That is what resilience looks like in action.

Resilience is built through ownership

I don’t think resilience is something you learn from a presentation or a leadership book.

It gets built layer by layer through challenges, setbacks and the decision to keep going.

That is also why ownership matters so much.

When something doesn’t go the way you wanted, you can spend your energy on everything outside your control.

Or you can ask:

What can I own, what can I change, and what am I going to do next?

I think the same question matters in business.

Markets change. Strategies miss. Deals fall apart. Products don’t always work the way we expected. Teams go through difficult periods.

The people I want around me aren’t the people who never experience setbacks.

They are the people who know what to do after one.

Why I look for resilience when I hire

Skills matter. Experience matters. Results matter.

But I also want to know how someone responds when the easy answer disappears.

Do they take ownership?

Can they adjust without losing confidence?

Do they keep pushing without blaming everyone around them?

Can they learn quickly enough to come back better?

Those qualities are difficult to capture on a résumé, but they become incredibly important when you’re building something new or leading through change.

Tommy reminds me of that constantly.

His resilience has made him a stronger athlete.

His drive keeps pushing him forward.

And watching him has made me think more carefully about the qualities I value in myself, the people I hire and the teams I build.

Setbacks don’t tell you who someone is. What they do next often does.
