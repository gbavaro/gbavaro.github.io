From Attention to Participation

Why the next commercial opportunity in media starts with what people do, not just what they watch.

Adapted from a LinkedIn post.

Guy BavaroOctober 11, 2026

For a long time, advertising was built around attention. Find the audience, reach them around the things they watch, and measure whether the message landed.

That still matters.

But I think the bigger commercial opportunity is increasingly being created by something else: participation.

People don’t just watch anymore. They play, predict, debate, create, compare, share and act while an experience is happening.

I’ve learned to pay attention to that behavior change before the business model fully shows up.

Start with what people are doing

The most useful question is not necessarily, “Where can we place advertising?”

It is:

What is the user doing in this moment, and can a brand add something useful to it?

That shifts the starting point.

Instead of beginning with a channel, an audience segment or an ad format, you begin with the behavior itself.

What is happening?

What does the person actually want to accomplish?

What are they participating in?

And has the brand earned the right to be there?

Intent and participation are starting to converge

AI makes this especially interesting.

People increasingly use AI to discover, compare, get advice, make decisions and complete tasks. That gives technology a much closer view of intent: what someone is trying to get done.

Gaming, sports and entertainment show another side of the same shift.

Fans don’t only watch. They play the matchup, argue the lineup, create content, make predictions and share reactions. That is participation: what someone cares enough to do.

I think the commercial opportunity becomes especially interesting where intent and participation meet.

That is the moment when a brand can either add value or get in the way.

A moment has to become buyable

The idea only becomes commercially useful when teams can plan, buy and operate against it.

It cannot just be an interesting observation about culture.

There has to be a way for advertisers and agencies to identify the moment, understand the user behavior, decide whether the brand belongs there and activate against it consistently.

That means the commercial model still has to connect to the systems the industry already uses: planning, creative, buying, measurement and optimization.

A new behavior becomes commercially important when the market can act on it repeatedly.

Measurement is the unfinished part

Reach and frequency are very good at answering one question: who saw something?

Participation creates another question:

Who actually did something?

Did someone play? Create? Share? Explore? Compare? Decide? Take an action that signals deeper involvement?

The industry is still building the measurement systems required to answer those questions consistently across platforms and experiences.

That is one of the most interesting commercial problems in front of us.

The business model usually follows the behavior

Technology changes quickly, but this pattern is familiar.

Behavior changes first.

Commercial interest follows.

Then the harder work begins: creating a product, an operating model and a measurement system that allow the market to adopt the opportunity at scale.

Attention is still valuable.

But I think the next generation of media businesses will increasingly be built around understanding something more important:

What people care enough to do.

That is where participation starts becoming a commercial model.
